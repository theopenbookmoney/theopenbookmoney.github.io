How this works

The interest rate differential (IRD) is your balance × the gap between your contract rate and today's comparable rate × the time left in your term. Three months' interest is your balance × your contract rate × 3/12. Your lender charges whichever is greater.

This is a simplified estimate. Real IRD calculations use posted rates and each lender's own formula, so two lenders can give two different numbers for the same mortgage. Variable-rate mortgages usually charge only three months' interest. Prepayment privileges (often 10–20% of the balance per year) can shrink the balance the penalty applies to.

Educational estimate: before fees and lender-specific rules. Before breaking a mortgage, ask your lender for the penalty in writing — that number governs, not this calculator.
