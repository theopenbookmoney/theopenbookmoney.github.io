The rules that matter

  • $8,000/year, $40,000 lifetime. Unused room carries forward. The account must close after 15 years or at age 71.
  • Qualifying withdrawal = tax-free. Buy your first home and the whole amount — contributions and growth — comes out with zero tax. That's the magic no RRSP or TFSA can match alone.
  • Not a first home? You can transfer the balance into your RRSP/RRIF tax-free (it doesn't use RRSP room). Only a non-qualifying cash withdrawal gets taxed.
  • You must be a first-time buyer — meaning you didn't own a home you lived in during this year or the previous four.
Assumptions
  • Contributions made at the start of each year; returns compound annually and are smooth (real markets wobble).
  • Lifetime contributions capped at $40,000 — the calculator stops counting there and tells you.
  • Tax saved = deductible contributions × your marginal rate. Assumes you have the income to deduct against.
  • Qualifying first-home withdrawal assumed — hence tax-free at exit.

Educational tool only, not financial advice. Verify current FHSA rules with CRA before contributing.
