The rules that matter

  • You must already receive OAS. Even a partial OAS pension counts — but if you delayed OAS, there is no GIS until OAS starts.
  • For every $2 of other income, GIS drops by about $1. A little income costs a little GIS; a lot costs all of it. Work is treated gently — see the employment note above.
  • File taxes every year, even with no income. Service Canada reviews GIS each July from last year's return. No return, no renewal.
  • It is tax-free and never repaid. GIS arrives in the same monthly deposit as OAS, for as long as you keep qualifying.
Assumptions
  • Figures are October–December 2026 Government of Canada maximums and income limits. Amounts change every quarter — the next update comes in January 2027.
  • This is an estimate using the simplified rule (about $1 of GIS lost per $2 of other income). The exact reduction rates vary by income bracket and situation, so your real amount comes from Service Canada's OAS estimator.
  • Income counted is last year's net income besides OAS, after the employment exemption. OAS itself never counts. TFSA withdrawals never count; RRSP and RRIF withdrawals count in full.
  • Assumes a full OAS pension. A partial OAS pension changes the numbers — contact Service Canada for your exact amount.
