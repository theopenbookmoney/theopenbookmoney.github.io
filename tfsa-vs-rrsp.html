TFSA vs RRSP

The real contest is not “tax-free” versus “tax-deferred.” It is your tax rate now versus your tax rate when the money comes out.

The one-line answer

If your tax rate is higher today than in retirement, the RRSP can pull ahead—provided you invest the full tax benefit. If the rates are equal, a properly grossed-up RRSP and TFSA are mathematically tied.

Start with the mechanism

A TFSA taxes the money before it goes in, then generally leaves the contribution, growth and withdrawal tax-free. An RRSP can reduce tax today, shelters growth inside the plan, and generally taxes withdrawals later.

TFSA

Pay tax now → invest after-tax dollars → withdraw tax-free.

No deduction for the contribution.
versus

RRSP

Deduct now → invest pre-tax dollars → pay tax on withdrawal.

The deduction’s value depends on your marginal tax rate.

The familiar mistake is to compare the same dollar contribution in each account and ignore the RRSP deduction. That gives the TFSA less money to work with. A fair comparison holds your after-tax cost constant.

TFSA future value = after-tax savings × growth
RRSP after-tax value = grossed-up savings × growth × (1 − withdrawal tax rate)
Tax rates equal? It is a tie.

With the same investments and fees, and the full RRSP tax benefit invested, the two structures deliver the same after-tax result when the contribution and withdrawal tax rates match.

A decision order that survives the slogans

  1. 01

    Compare marginal rates

    If today’s rate is above your expected withdrawal rate, the RRSP gets a structural advantage. If it is below, the TFSA gets it. Equal rates point to a tie before other considerations.

  2. 02

    Be honest about the refund

    The RRSP advantage depends on investing the tax benefit. Spending the refund breaks the equal-cost comparison and leaves less money compounding.

  3. 03

    Price flexibility

    TFSA withdrawals are generally tax-free, and the amount withdrawn is added back to contribution room the next calendar year. That flexibility can be valuable well before retirement.

  4. 04

    Consider benefit interactions

    TFSA income and withdrawals do not affect federal income-tested benefits or credits. RRSP withdrawals are generally taxable, so they can increase the income used in benefit calculations.

  5. 05

    Use your actual room

    Contribution limits are personal. Check your records and CRA information before depositing; this page does not calculate available contribution room.
