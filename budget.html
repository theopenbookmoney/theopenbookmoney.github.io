Your Paycheque Has a Leak

October 2026

Rob is inspired by a real person — names and details changed so the story can be told honestly. Every number in this article is a clearly labelled assumption you can change in the calculator below.

The mystery of the missing money

Rob and Priya earn decent money. Warehouse nights for him. Nursing shifts for her. Two incomes. Not rich, but fine.

So why did the savings account never grow?

Every month the money came in. Every month it went out. At the end, almost nothing was left. Nobody could say where it went. It just... leaked.

Rob said it out loud one night: "We earn decent money. Where does it go?"

Priya did what she always does. She opened the spreadsheet.

The leaks

She pulled three months of bank statements and read every line. It took an hour. What she found was not one big problem. It was a hundred small ones.

Subscriptions nobody uses. Three streaming services. They watch one. A gym membership from January. It is October. A meal-kit box that shows up every Tuesday and gets half eaten.

Food delivery markups. The pizza is $18 on the menu. On the app it is $24, plus a $5 fee, plus a tip on the bigger number. An $18 pizza becomes a $33 pizza. Twice a week.

Impulse buys. The phone case. The sale rack. The "add to cart" at 11 p.m. Each one is small. Small times fifty is not small.

Lifestyle creep. This one is quiet. The raise comes. Spending rises to meet it. The old car worked fine, but now there is a payment. Nothing feels different. But the savings rate stays at zero.

None of these is a crime. That is what makes them dangerous. Nobody notices a leak. You notice a flood.

The 50/30/20 rule

Priya showed Rob a simple rule. Fancy name: the 50/30/20 rule. Simple idea: split your pay into three buckets.

It works on your take-home pay — what actually lands in your account, not the number on your job offer. (That is a whole other article.)

Here it is:

  • 50% for needs. The bills that do not stop. Rent or mortgage. Groceries. Utilities. Transport. Minimum debt payments.
  • 30% for wants. Everything you choose. Restaurants. Clothes. Streaming. The fun stuff.
  • 20% for savings. Emergency fund. Investing. Extra debt payments. Future-you money.

One central idea: every dollar gets a job before you spend it.

The worked example: Rob's numbers

Labelled assumptions — swap in your own below. Rob's household take-home pay: $4,000 a month.

  • Needs: 50% → $2,000
  • Wants: 30% → $1,200
  • Savings: 20% → $800

Read that last number. $800 a month is $9,600 a year. That is an emergency fund in about 18 months, or a serious head start on anything else.

Now compare it to the leaks. Priya added them up: the unused subscriptions, the delivery pizzas, the 11 p.m. carts. $650 a month. Almost the entire savings number, leaking out in pieces nobody chose on purpose.

The honest part: 50/30/20 is a starting point

Scenario honesty, the way this site works. The rule is a starting point, not a law.

50/30/20 works great IF:

  • Your housing costs are sane. In Saskatoon, maybe. In Toronto or Vancouver, 50% for needs might be a fantasy.
  • Your income covers the basics with room to spare.

It does not fit IF:

  • You earn very little. When rent alone is 60% of take-home, the rule just makes you feel bad. Skip the percentages. Save something, even $25 a month.
  • You are in a debt emergency. Then the 20% goes to war against the expensive debt first — credit cards, payday loans — before it goes anywhere near a savings account.
  • Your income is lumpy. Freelancers, seasonal workers — some months are fat, some are thin. A fixed split fights reality.

The alternative: zero-based budgeting

For the people the rule does not fit, there is a second way. Scary name. Simple idea.

Zero-based budgeting: before the month starts, give every dollar a job. Income minus every planned expense equals zero. Not zero money left — zero unassigned dollars.

It takes about 30 minutes on the 1st. Rent: $1,400. Groceries: $700. Everything gets a line. When the dollars run out, the spending plan stops. That is the whole trick.

Zero-based is more work than 50/30/20. But it fits any income, any city, any life. It is the custom suit to the rule's off-the-rack.

Find your leaks in one hour

You do not need a system yet. You need one hour and the truth. Here is the exercise:

  1. Pull one month of bank and card statements.
  2. Get three highlighters. Or three pen colours. Or three piles.
  3. Mark every line: need, want, or leak — a want you did not even enjoy.
  4. Add up the leaks.

That number is your raise. You do not need a new job. You need to plug the hole.

Rob and Priya's leak total was $650 a month. They kept the one streaming service they actually watch. Delivery became a Friday thing. The gym membership died with dignity. Six months later, the savings account held $4,800. The leaks became the emergency fund.

The bottom line

Your paycheque is probably not too small. It is leaking. Find the leaks, give every dollar a job, and the same income builds a completely different life.
