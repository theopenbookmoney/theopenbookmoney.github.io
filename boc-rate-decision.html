Bank of Canada Rate Decisions: What Changed and What to Do About It

Next decision: Wednesday, October 28, 2026 (9:45 ET)

Where we stand going in: the overnight rate sits at 2.25%, held seven straight times — the Bank hasn't moved since cutting to 2.25% last October. Headline inflation was 3.0% year-over-year in August (reported Sept 14), at the top of the Bank's 1–3% control range, with prices excluding gasoline up 2.4%, from 2.2% in July — a sign pressure outside energy was building. The new complication is expectations: in a Nanos poll for Bloomberg conducted Sept 27–29 (n=1,057, accurate to ±3 points 19 times in 20), 54% of Canadians expected inflation to be higher a year from now than the current 3%, about a third expected it to be about the same, and only 7% expected it lower.

What to watch before the 28th — the remaining releases: 1. Fri Oct 9 — September jobs (Statistics Canada): landed. The last labour read before the decision. The full labour-market update folds into this page with the Oct 19 data below. 2. Mon Oct 19 — September CPI (8:30 ET) and the Bank's Business Outlook Survey + Canadian Survey of Consumer Expectations (11:30 ET). Same-day double hit: actual inflation and whether businesses/households are baking it in. This is the expectations test. 3. Wed Oct 28, 9:45 ET — decision + Monetary Policy Report. Whether the MPR upgrades the inflation forecast is still the tell.

The consensus going in — no longer a clean hold: the Sept 30 framing (hold #8 as base case) is stale. Overnight swaps priced the odds of a hike on Oct 28 at roughly one in three at the start of October; after the September jobs report landed, market pricing moved to about one in four, with more than 100 basis points of hikes still priced over the next 12 months. Economists are more cautious than traders — the split itself is the story. Governor Macklem has warned that moving too slowly on sustained inflation could force faster, larger hikes later, which is why a single hot CPI or expectations print can move this meeting.

What to do about it (pre-decision)

  • Variable-rate mortgage or line of credit? Don't budget for relief. The live risk for Oct 28 is now a hike, not a cut — prime is 4.45% today and would be expected at 4.70% the day after a 0.25-point hike. If you've been waiting to refinance or accelerate payments, run the math at today's rate, not a hoped-for lower one.
  • Parking cash in a HISA or GIC? A hold at 2.25% means promo rates are the whole game right now (see the cash-parking guide). A hike would lift prime-linked yields first; posted savings rates still lag without competitive pressure.
  • Fixed mortgage coming up for renewal? Fixed rates follow bond markets, not the Bank — seven holds didn't stop them from drifting on their own, and hike pricing will move them before Oct 28 if Oct 19 data runs hot. Shop the rate, not the announcement.

The evergreen rule

The Bank of Canada doesn't set your mortgage rate — it sets the weather your rate lives in. Variable rates move with the overnight rate; fixed rates move with bond markets' expectations of where it's going. That's why the Monetary Policy Report matters more than the number: the number tells you today, the report tells you the direction.
