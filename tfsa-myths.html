Your TFSA Is Not a Savings Account

October 2026

Jasmine is inspired by a real person — names and details changed so the story can be told honestly. Every number in this article is real 2026 data.

The withdrawal that almost cost Jasmine $30 a month

Jasmine opened her first TFSA in January. Priya nagged her into it. "It's tax-free," Priya said. "Just put something in."

So Jasmine did. She put in $7,000 — the full 2026 limit — and forgot about it. In her head, it was a savings account. A slightly better one. Money goes in, money comes out, no big deal.

In March, her car needed a $3,000 repair. She pulled $3,000 out of the TFSA. No tax, no forms, money in her account in two days. Easy.

In June, overtime was good. She put the $3,000 back in. Room restored, she thought. Like refilling a glass.

That night she mentioned it to Priya. Priya went quiet — the bad kind of quiet.

"You just over-contributed," Priya said. "The room doesn't come back until January. The CRA is going to charge you 1% a month on that $3,000 until you take it back out or January comes."

Jasmine stared at her. "But it's a savings account. I put money in and took money out. That's what savings accounts do."

And that is exactly the problem. The TFSA is not a savings account. The name lies to you. This article is about the three ways that lie costs people real money.

Myth 1: It's an account. It's not. It's a wrapper.

Here is the first thing nobody explains: TFSA is not a type of account. It is a label. A tax wrapper.

Think of it like a sticker. The government lets you put a "tax-free" sticker on certain investments. Whatever sits under the sticker grows without tax. Whatever sits outside the sticker doesn't.

Under the sticker you can hold:

  • Cash and savings
  • GICs
  • Bonds
  • Stocks
  • ETFs and mutual funds

So when someone says "I have a TFSA savings account," what they really mean is: "I put a savings account under the tax-free sticker." That's allowed. But it's like buying a toolbox and only ever storing one screwdriver in it. The sticker works on stocks and ETFs too — and over decades, that's where the tax-free part actually matters, because growth is what gets taxed outside the sticker.

The name — Tax-Free Savings Account — makes everyone picture a savings account. The government named a sports car "grocery getter." Don't let the name shrink your thinking.

Myth 2: The room comes back when you withdraw. It doesn't — not this year.

This is the trap that caught Jasmine. And it catches thousands of Canadians every year. Here is the rule, in plain words:

When you take money out of a TFSA, you do NOT get that room back right away. It comes back on January 1 of the next year.

Not next month. Not next week. Next January.

So if you put money back in during the same year — and you had no spare room — you have over-contributed. The withdrawal and the re-contribution are two separate events, and the calendar doesn't care that it's "the same money."

The worked example: Jasmine's $30-a-month mistake

Labelled assumptions: in 2026, Jasmine's available TFSA room was exactly $7,000, and she contributed the full $7,000 in January.
Contributed in January $7,000
Room left $0
Withdrew in March (car repair) $3,000 — tax-free, no problem so far
Room back from that withdrawal $0 — not until January 1, 2027
Re-contributed in June $3,000 — but room is still $0
Over-contribution $3,000

The penalty: 1% per month on the excess, for every month it sits there. $3,000 × 1% = $30 a month. If it sits from June through December — seven months — that's $210 in penalty tax. On her own money, going back into her own account.

The fix Priya gave her: take the $3,000 back out right away. The monthly clock stops. Then wait for January 1, when the room returns, and put it back in. Jasmine paid for two months before she caught it. $60. An expensive lesson with a cheap tuition, as Priya put it.

And if you do it on purpose? The CRA has a second, harsher rule. If you deliberately over-contribute — knowingly stuffing extra money in to farm tax-free growth — the CRA can tax the gains at 100%. Every dollar of growth on the excess, taken back. The 1% monthly charge is for mistakes. The 100% charge is for schemes. Don't be a scheme.

Myth 3: "CRA My Account says I have room, so I have room."

Jasmine's second near-miss came in October. She logged into CRA My Account. It showed she had room. She almost contributed again.

Priya stopped her. "That number is stale."

Here is what the CRA itself says: the TFSA room figure in My Account is only updated once a year, in the spring, after every bank and brokerage files its annual report. Your contributions and withdrawals from this year are not in there yet. The CRA says the best time to check is after April — and even then, you should verify against your own records.

So the number on screen in February can easily show room you already used up. The CRA's own example: a woman checked in January, saw room, contributed in February — and ended up $4,000 over the limit because the screen hadn't caught up to her real activity.

The rule: keep your own running total. A note on your phone. A line in a spreadsheet. Contributions in, withdrawals out, across every TFSA you hold at every institution — because the limit is shared across all your accounts, not per account. The CRA will not warn you before you go over. They will notify you in late spring, with a bill.

The numbers that are real

Verified 2026 figures, so you have the anchors:

  • 2026 annual limit: $7,000. New room arrives every January 1.
  • Lifetime maximum: $109,000 — if you were 18 or older when the TFSA launched in 2009 and never contributed a dollar. Your own number depends on when you turned 18; room starts accumulating that year.
  • Unused room never expires. It carries forward forever.
  • Withdrawals are always tax-free and never count as income. They just don't restore room until January.

The bottom line

Three sentences to tape to the fridge:

  1. A TFSA is a tax-free sticker, not a savings account. Put real investments under it.
  2. Money you take out doesn't free up room until January 1. Put it back early and you pay 1% a month.
  3. The CRA's screen is months behind. Track your own room or pay for the difference.

Jasmine keeps a note on her phone now: "TFSA in: $7,000. TFSA out: $3,000. Room back: Jan 1." She checks it before she touches the account. The sticker is powerful — but only if you read the fine print on it.
